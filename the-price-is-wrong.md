### 0.  Introduction
#### 0. 
- "If price indeed drives energy technology adoption, and if renewables are now cheapest, why is government economic support for renewables still so important - or at least, still seen to be indispensable?"
#### I. 
- "...to look predominantly at price when thinking about what makes for a successful (or failed) transition is to look in the wrong place."
- "The better, more meaningful yardstick is profit... most governments worldwide have effectively outsourced responsibility for developing, owning and operating solar and wind farms to profit-oriented private sector actors, and yet the profits that such actors expect to be able to earn from investments in these activities generally underwhelm."
- "...there are all sorts of reasons why lower costs of generation do not automatically equate to higher profits."
#### II. 
- "Already, in other words, we are far off the pace of investment in clean power that is required if we are going to have any reasonable prospect of making good on our historic failures."
#### III.
- "As the Economist put it in 2022, the world's current decarbonisation strategy can effective by captured in two words: 'Electrify everything'."
- "But our focus will be on the large, commercially oriented, 'utility-scale' renewables developments that, ultimately, will make or break most countries' attempts to decarbonise electricity."
- "When a commercial entity makes a decision about a potential investment in, say, new wind power capacity, the choice is almost always between wind farm and no wind farm, not wind farm and gas-fired plant, and thus the relevant profitability benchmark is the rate of return that the investor considers acceptable rather than the rate of return on dirty generation assets."

#### VI.

### 2. The Business of Power
#### 0.
- The transition into a state of neoliberal electricity market comes in the form of unbundling (of national electricity sectors), de-monopolisation, privatisation, and marketisation. 
#### I. 
- "But the basic story of electricity is the basic story of 'neoliberalisation' - the amalgam of processes whereby neoliberalism has effectively been engineered on the ground."
#### II. Unbundling
- "The tendency for generation and other core industry activities increasingly to be separated from one another, and for the vertically integrated entities... to be dis-integrated"
- "Wind- and solar-based electricity generation is in large measure a discrete, standalone business".
#### III. De-monopolosation
- "Vertical unbundling was considered necessary - that is, to facilitate competition."
    - "Companies could not be expected to compete effectively in one business area if lumbered with another, especially if the latter activity was not itself amenable to competition"
    - "Conflicts of interest could arise if governments were trying to engender competition without having first unbundled."
#### IV. Privatisation
- "Insofar as the private sector is ruled by the profit imperative, profitability - its determinants, availability and scale - utterly dominates renewable energy's conditions of both possibility and performance."
#### V. Marketisation
- "The turning over of sectoral transactions of all sorts to theretically unfettered exchange between market actors."
#### VI. 
- "The key learning is sthat the generation of electrcity is increasingly a seperaet business"
- "As well as using being separate from the rest of the supply chain, electricity generation is usuaully competitive - much moreso than any of transmission, distribution or retail. The entities that compete in electricity generation, and especially renewables generation, are also typically proviate corporations, existing to generate profit."
- "It is more and more the case that what types of generation capacity installed, how much electrcity is then produced, and how much the customers of generating companies pay for that electricity are all determined by market mechanisms of various kionds, trather than by, say, direct, top-down government perspcription.
#### Other.

#### I. 
- "The critical factor to consider is the relative price paid for power: if electricity can be generated more cheaply from renewable sources than from fossil fuels, then the former will surge ahead; but if fossil-fuel-based power is cheaper to produce, then it, instead, will remain dominant."
#### II. 
- "The 'economic style of reasining' took over... environmental problems increasingly came to be seen as economic problems. Moreso specifically still, they came to be seen as price problems."
- "Historians have found that for a 'full' energy transition from one energy form to another to take place, the price of the winning technology must be lower... only one factor - a lower price - is necessary for the transition to occur."
#### III. Government intervention
- "Support investment - esentially the construction cost"
- "Financial support... in many countries, the market cost of borrowing to develop renewables is punitive, and thus without a mechanism for lowering that cost, financing typically representsa a major - frequently insurmountable - barrier to project realissation."
- Indirect revenue support via demand stimulation/obligations
- Incremental revenue stream specifically for renewably generated energy
- Price control
#### IV. 
- There has been explicit strategy in developing solar manufacturing capabilities by the Chinese state, in addition to international demand.
- "As it became cheaper and cheaper to produce solar and wind equipment... it became cheaper and cheaper to generate electricity using such equipment."
#### V. 
- At a certain point (2019), there was thoughts that renewables had become the cheapest form of generation, critically, without intervention/subsidies. 
#### VI. Why renewables did not overtake fossil fuels, despite being cheaper
- "Bureaucracy: esentially, slow permitting, slow grid connection, or a combination foe each"
- "Through their influence on policy-makers, companies in and around the coal and natural gas sectors are succeeding in forestalling the decarbonisation fo electricity."
- "The main remianing barrier to change is the ability of incumbents to manipulate political forces to stop change."
